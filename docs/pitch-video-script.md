# AskWise — Pitch Video Script (FINAL v2)

> UNESCO Youth Hackathon 2026 · Team THE TANK · max 3:00
> Sửa từ bản v1 của Ngọc. Danh sách thay đổi + việc cần xác minh ở cuối file.

## ACT 1 — The Problem (0:00 – 0:55)

### 0:00–0:08 · Cold open — the message
**VISUAL:** Full-screen mock-up of a phone chat on near-black (`#050A30`). A message types itself out in real time: “Remote data entry. $20/day. No experience needed. Contact via Telegram only.” Cursor blinks in cyan (`#00FFFF`). Everything else is black.
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** *No text overlay yet — let the message land alone*
**EDIT NOTE:** Silence except a single soft notification chime. Starting with no voiceover for 8 seconds is a deliberate hook; resist the urge to fill it. The background is the product's actual base colour — the video and the site now share one palette, so this frame already "belongs" to AskWise before the logo appears.

### 0:08–0:20 · The universal moment
**VISUAL:** Slow push-in on the same message. Cut to stock footage: a young person's hands holding a phone in a dim room, face not visible (search: “student phone night”, “hands smartphone dark”).
**VOICEOVER:** *“You have seen a message like this. Maybe you paused for a second. Something felt slightly off — but you were not sure what, or how to check.”*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** **Something feels off. Now what?**
**EDIT NOTE:** Cut on the word 'paused'. The second-person address pulls the jury into the experience rather than presenting them with a case study.

### 0:20–0:33 · The one statistic that hits
**VISUAL:** Black screen. A single number counts up to 64% in cyan and holds, very large. Then the second half of the sentence appears beneath it in smaller white type. No other cards, no clutter.
**VOICEOVER:** *“We surveyed thirty-nine young people in Viet Nam. Sixty-four per cent could tell a posting like this was too good to be true. And of those, sixty-four per cent did nothing to check it. Not one search.”*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** **64% SENSED IT · OF THOSE, 64% CHECKED NOTHING**
*(small source line: Team survey, n=39, Viet Nam, July 2026)*
**EDIT NOTE:** ⚠️ **Verify both figures are really 64% before recording** — two identical percentages in one sentence reads like a copy-paste error to a jury, which costs more credibility than the stat gains. If they genuinely are both 64%, say "of those" clearly so the nesting is audible. Mentor guidance was explicit: one stat, not four. Supporting figures (91% job-hunt on social media, 87% never trained) may appear as small text at the lower third for 2 seconds, but must NOT be narrated. Drop the music out entirely for the last 2 seconds of this shot.

### 0:33–0:45 · The heartbeat — where the idea came from
**VISUAL:** Mock-up of a student group chat scrolling: several people reacting to a forwarded job posting with opinions, nobody with an answer. Messages appear in quick succession, then the thread simply stops.
**VOICEOVER:** *“This started in our own group chat. A classmate forwarded a posting and asked if it was real. Twelve people had an opinion. Nobody had a method. The thread just ended.”*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** **Nobody had a method.**
**EDIT NOTE:** TEAM: replace this with your real trigger if it differs — a true specific story outperforms a polished general one, and this is the beat that makes the jury believe you. Keep it under 13 seconds; it is a heartbeat, not a chapter.

### 0:45–0:55 · Name the real problem
**VISUAL:** Text-only card, generous dark space, one line at a time. Then a subtle animation: a static checklist graphic ('10 Signs of a Job Scam') fades up and dissolves into dust.
**VOICEOVER:** *“This is not carelessness. It is an untrained skill. Awareness campaigns say be careful. Checklists say what to look for. Neither teaches anyone how to actually check.”*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** **Suspicion without a method.**
**EDIT NOTE:** Hold 'Suspicion without a method.' for a full 2 seconds in silence before Act 2 begins. This phrase is the spine of the whole pitch — it should also appear in the proposal and in your live Q&A answers.

## ACT 2 — The Solution (0:55 – 2:05)

### 0:55–1:05 · Introduce the product — the one-sentence test
**VISUAL:** Music lifts and warms. Logo animation: **AskWise**. Cut to a clean screen recording of the live site loading in a browser, URL bar visible (`ask-wise-rose.vercel.app`).
**VOICEOVER:** *“So we built AskWise: an AI tutor that refuses to tell you whether a posting is a scam, and instead asks you six questions until you work it out yourself.”*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** **An AI tutor that refuses to give you the answer**
**EDIT NOTE:** This is the sentence a judge must be able to repeat afterwards — deliver it slowly and cleanly, and match the on-screen text to the narration word for word. Keep the URL bar visible for one beat: it quietly proves the product is deployed and public.

### 1:05–1:40 · The live demo — the core of the video
**VISUAL:** Unbroken screen recording of a real session. Pick the scenario, then show the tutor's first question, one user reply, and the tutor's follow-up. Zoom gently on each new message as it appears.
**VOICEOVER:** *“You bring in the posting. Instead of a verdict, you get a question. Then another, built on what you just said. Six steps, in a fixed order, taken straight from the verification method taught in the UNESCO mentoring sessions: pause, trace the source, spot the signs, reverse-search, check the primary source, and name what you still cannot know.”*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** Overlay the six step names as small cyan chips, lighting up in sequence as the voiceover names them. Small credit line, lower right: *'Six-step method: [SPEAKER NAME], UNESCO Youth Hackathon Mentoring Session, 2 July 2026'*
**EDIT NOTE:** ⚠️ **Confirm the mentor's full name spelling before recording** — the v1 script wrote "Elizabeth Sherr" but the session notes only record the first name. Crediting a real person with a wrong surname on screen is worse than no credit. The credit line itself matters: it shows the jury you applied the programme rather than inventing a framework. Record the session three or four times and pick the cleanest run. Speed the recording to roughly 1.5x with smooth motion, but never speed up the moment a question appears.

### 1:40–1:55 · The two steps that matter most
**VISUAL:** Split screen: left, the survey figures 18% and 23% in muted grey-blue; right, the tutor's screen showing the reverse-search and primary-source questions in full colour.
**VOICEOVER:** *“Two of those steps — searching independently, and checking the official source — are exactly the two almost nobody in our survey performed. So we made sure the tutor never lets you skip them.”*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** **Steps 4 and 5: the missing habits**
**EDIT NOTE:** ⚠️ Line changed from v1. The old line claimed *"that is where the tutor spends the most time"* — **that is not true of the built product**: all six steps get equal weight and at most one follow-up each. A judge can open the site and check in ninety seconds. The new wording claims only what the fixed six-step order actually guarantees. This shot does the analytical heavy lifting: it proves the design was driven by the data rather than by guesswork.

### 1:55–2:05 · The payoff — the user reaches the answer
**VISUAL:** Screen recording of the closing exchange: the user types their own summary, then the tutor's recap appears. Highlight the final line about MIL with a soft cyan glow.
**VOICEOVER:** *“Only after you have reasoned it out yourself does the tutor give its honest take — and tell you that what you just practised has a name.”*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** **“What you just practised is called media and information literacy.”**
**EDIT NOTE:** Let the final line sit on screen for 2 seconds. This is the emotional resolution of the arc opened in the cold open.

## ACT 3 — Impact and Ask (2:05 – 2:56)

> Timecodes re-cut: v1 had 2:05–2:20 overlapping 2:16–2:26. Blocks below are contiguous.

### 2:05–2:17 · Beyond job scams
**VISUAL:** Motion graphic: the six-step wheel at centre in cyan on prussian. Four domain labels orbit into place around it — Job scams (highlighted), Health claims, Deepfakes, Phishing.
**VOICEOVER:** *“The engine does not care what it is examining. The same six questions work on a fake health cure, a deepfake video, or a phishing message. New scenarios, same method.”*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** **One engine. Any manipulation pattern.**
**EDIT NOTE:** Reuse the exact wheel graphic from the proposal so the two documents visually reinforce each other. Build it in the site palette (Prussian `#050A30` / Cyan `#00FFFF` / Blue `#0000FF`).

### 2:17–2:27 · Who this has to reach
**VISUAL:** Map graphic of Viet Nam and the wider region, with soft cyan markers spreading outward. Then a two-panel shot of the interface — Vietnamese on the left, English on the right — the same question in both.
**VOICEOVER:** *“And it has to work for the people most exposed, not just for university students. Vocational students. Young people moving to a new city for work. Anyone who has been offered a job abroad that sounded too generous to refuse.”*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** **Vietnamese and English · No account · No install · Free to use**
**EDIT NOTE:** ⚠️ `Works offline` removed from v1 — **it was false.** AskWise calls a hosted model on every turn; with no connection it does nothing. A claim a judge can disprove by turning off wi-fi is not worth four words. Also: the two-panel VI/EN shot is only honest once the English interface actually ships — if it is not live by filming day, show a mock-up and keep the on-screen text as a stated commitment. Do not show trafficking imagery or victim footage of any kind.

### 2:27–2:33 · Community and reach
**VISUAL:** Stock footage: a university classroom or student club meeting, warm natural light (search: “students workshop classroom Asia”, “young people laptop group”). Overlay simple icons: a scenario library growing, entries being added.
**VOICEOVER:** *“Our next step is a scenario library built by students themselves — real cases from real group chats, not textbook examples.”*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** **A library built by the people it protects**
**EDIT NOTE:** Choose footage with Asian students where possible; a mismatch between the narration's Viet Nam context and visibly Western stock footage undercuts credibility.

### 2:33–2:41 · The ask
**VISUAL:** Clean typographic slide, three short lines appearing in sequence over a soft prussian-to-blue gradient.
**VOICEOVER:** *“What we need is not funding. It is classrooms to test in, and MIL educators to tell us where our questions are wrong.”*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** **WHAT WE NEED: Classrooms to test in · MIL mentors to challenge our method · Scenarios from other countries**
**EDIT NOTE:** Most pitch videos skip the ask entirely — the mentoring session listed it as a must-have. Naming a non-financial need signals maturity and that the project is already running.

### 2:41–2:51 · The closing statement
**VISUAL:** Return to the opening scam message on the phone — same framing as the cold open. This time, the six step chips light up across it one by one in cyan, and the message no longer feels intimidating.
**VOICEOVER:** *“We cannot stop these messages from arriving. But we can make sure that the next time one does, you know exactly what to do.”*
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** **Don't tell them the answer. Teach them the question.**
**EDIT NOTE:** The callback to the opening shot closes the loop. This is the line the jury should still remember an hour later — make sure it also appears in your proposal's conclusion.

### 2:51–2:56 · End card
**VISUAL:** Clean end card on prussian: project name, team name, university, live URL, and a small disclaimer line.
**ON-SCREEN TEXT:** **ASKWISE · Team THE TANK · University of Economics Ho Chi Minh City · ask-wise-rose.vercel.app · UNESCO Youth Hackathon 2026**
**EDIT NOTE:** Small grey disclaimer at the bottom: *'All scam messages shown are recreations. No real personal data is displayed.'* Music resolves and fades out on the last frame.

## Cần xác minh trước khi quay

- [ ] **Hai con số 64%** có thật sự đều là 64% không? Trùng số trong cùng một câu rất dễ bị đọc là lỗi copy-paste, mất uy tín nhiều hơn cái lợi của số liệu.
- [ ] **Họ tên đầy đủ của diễn giả** cho dòng credit ở 1:05–1:40. Bản v1 ghi "Elizabeth Sherr" nhưng ghi chú webinar chỉ có tên "Elizabeth". Ghi sai họ của một người thật lên màn hình còn tệ hơn không ghi.
- [ ] **Giao diện tiếng Anh** có kịp live trước ngày quay không — cảnh hai khung VI/EN ở 2:17 và dòng chữ "Vietnamese and English" phụ thuộc vào việc này.
- [ ] Sáu bước trong lời thoại **đã đối chiếu khớp** với `SYSTEM_PROMPT` thật của sản phẩm ✅ (pause / trace source / spot signs / reverse-search / primary source / transparency).
